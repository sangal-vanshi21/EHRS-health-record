1. Problem Statement (5W1H Framework)

Who & What: Healthcare providers (physicians, nurses, specialists) and patients across hospital networks struggle with fragmented clinical data trapped in disparate paper charts and isolated software silos.

When & Where: During outpatient consultations, emergency triage, and prescription dispensing at hospital and community clinic points-of-care.

Why: The lack of a centralized, real-time clinical platform leads to repeated diagnostic testing, missed drug-allergy cross-reactivity contraindications, diagnostic turnaround delays, and minimal patient involvement in care plans.

How: By engineering a centralized, web-based Electronic Health Record System (EHRS01) featuring Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE), structured SOAP documentation, dynamic BMI monitoring, automated real-time allergy cross-checks, bidirectional HL7 v2.5.1 and FHIR R4 interoperability bridges, and a self-service Patient Portal.

2. Proposed Solution & Approach

The developed solution is an enterprise full-stack Electronic Health Record System built with modern React 19, Tailwind CSS, and a Node.js/Express service architecture adhering to global healthcare interoperability standards. The platform implements six core clinical epics: Master Patient Index (MPI) demographics management, structured 4-quadrant SOAP encounter documentation with automatic BMI computation, Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE) for laboratory and imaging diagnostics with an integrated PACS DICOM viewer simulation, electronic prescribing (e-Rx) with real-time Clinical Decision Support (CDS) allergy cross-checking, and bidirectional HL7 v2.5.1 messaging alongside HL7 FHIR Release 4 REST endpoints. Furthermore, a dedicated Patient Portal enables self-scheduling of appointments, secure encrypted provider messaging, and instant personal health record access, thereby streamlining clinical throughput and safeguarding patient care.

5. Dataset Used & Clinical Schema

Dataset Source: Synthea-derived synthetic clinical cohorts and clinical documentation guidelines (`initialData.js`).

Record Volume: 4 comprehensive master patient profiles representing acute, chronic, and post-operative clinical cohorts; 14 clinical encounters with signed SOAP notes; 8 CPOE laboratory and imaging diagnostic orders; 12 active/discontinued electronic medication orders; 6 patient portal appointments; 4 hospital connector interfaces; 20 agile sprint metrics.

Key Clinical Attributes (5 Primary): 1. mrn / patientId (Master Patient Index), 2. allergies (Allergen, reaction, severity for CDS), 3. vitals (BP, HR, SpO2, temp, auto BMI), 4. loincCode / results (Analyte codes, reference intervals, flags), 5. rxNormCode / dosage (Pharmaceutical codes, frequency, route).

7. Challenges Faced & How Resolved

Challenge 1: Real-Time Drug-Allergy Cross-Reactivity Problem: Prescribing software often fails to catch cross-reactive antibiotics (e.g., prescribing Amoxicillin to patient allergic to "Penicillin").
Resolution: Engineered a multi-tiered contraindication rule engine mapping drugs against exact allergens and pharmacological classes with an interactive clinical override modal requiring documented physician justification.
Challenge 2: Dual Legacy HL7 v2.5.1 & FHIR R4 Standards Problem: Hospitals rely on legacy pipe-delimited HL7 v2, while modern web apps require RESTful FHIR R4 JSON resources.
Resolution: Designed a modular transformation layer comprising an ER7 tokenizer/generator for standard HL7 segments (MSH, PID, PV1, OBR, OBX, RXE) and parallel JSON transformers exposing standard FHIR endpoints.
